Lesson 5

How urban is Vietnam?

By the official definition, of the people of Vietnam lived in urban areas in 2020. By the international Degree of Urbanisation, lived in cities, towns or semi-dense areas. This lesson explains why the two figures differ, maps where they disagree, and then follows the spread of cities with the lights that satellites record at night — the measures that the World Bank used in its 2020 review of urbanisation in Vietnam.

Data: the urban share of the World Development Indicators; the Degree of Urbanisation grid and tables (GHS-SMOD and GHS-DUC R2023A) of the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre; a harmonised series of night-time lights for 1992–2024; boundaries from geoBoundaries. Numbers in the text are produced by kich_ban/l5_do_thi_hoa.py.

Learning objectives

After this lesson you should be able to:

  1. explain how the official definition of urban areas in Vietnam is drawn and how it differs from the Degree of Urbanisation;
  2. read a map of the Degree of Urbanisation and explain why so much of Vietnam falls into the class of towns and semi-dense areas;
  3. use night-time lights to follow the spread of cities, and give two reasons why a series of lights can mislead.

5.2Where the definitions disagree

Map 5.1 shows the grid behind these shares. Red cells belong to urban centres; the shades of brown and yellow belong to urban clusters, which make up the towns and semi-dense areas; the greens are rural.

In the deltas, settlement is dense enough for the cells between towns to reach the threshold of an urban cluster, and the yellow of suburban and peri-urban cells spreads across the countryside. Cities and urban clusters cover of the land of the Red River Delta and of the Mekong Delta, against in the rest of the country outside the South East. Table 5.1 adds up the population by class. In the Red River Delta only of the people live in cells classified as rural, and even outside the three regions shown the rural share is only . Officially, of the people of Vietnam were rural in 2020.

Interpretation — which definition is right?The definitions answer different questions. The official definition records administrative status, which decides how a place is planned and governed. The Degree of Urbanisation records how densely people live, and in the deltas of Vietnam many villages are as dense as the suburbs of other countries. For the monocentric model, what matters is neither status nor density as such, but whether land is bid for by households who work in a city: none of the three measures observes that directly.

5.3Lights at night

To measure the growth of urban land, the World Bank used images of light at night, counting as urban the cells brighter than a threshold:

With this method it found that the spread of urban land had sped up:

Satellites have recorded night-time light since 1992, but not with one instrument. Until 2013 the images come from the DMSP satellites, whose sensor saturates in bright city centres; the VIIRS instrument that followed is far more sensitive. Li et al. (2020) converted the VIIRS images to the scale of the DMSP to build a single series from 1992, now extended to 2024. Map 5.2 and Figure 5.2 use it.

Measured in this series with the same threshold, the lit share of the land of Vietnam rose from in 1996 to in 2012 and in 2017: lower than the report’s figures, but of the same order. The rates of growth tell a different story. Within the years of each instrument, the lit area grew by a year from 1996 to 2013 and by a year from 2014 to 2024: growth slowed rather than sped up. The step from 2013 to 2014, where the two instruments meet, is , more than twice the typical change between two years (). Measured across the change of instrument, as in the report, the rate does rise: from a year in 1996–2012 to in 2012–2017.

Interpretation — to be tested with other dataPart of the acceleration reported in 2020 may come from the change of satellite rather than from the land. The harmonised series is itself an estimate, built by converting one instrument into the other, so this lesson cannot settle the question. It shows that the answer depends on how the two instruments are joined, and that a growth rate measured across the join needs a check from a source that does not change, such as the built-up land of Lesson 4.

Lights also measure something other than land. Within 30 km of Ben Thanh, the area lit above the threshold grew times between 1992 and 2015, while built-up land measured from Landsat grew times; around Hoan Kiem Lake the factors are and . Land that was already built up became brighter, and a lit area counts every cell above the threshold, whether it is covered by buildings or by a single lit road.

Finally, light has spread away from the two largest cities (Figure 5.2, right). The share of the country’s light emitted within 50 km of Ben Thanh or of Hoan Kiem Lake fell from in 1992 to in 2013, and from in 2014 to in 2024 — a fall within each instrument, not an effect of the join.

5.4What this means for the model

The monocentric model places the edge of a city where the rent that city households bid for land falls to the rent of farmland (Lesson 1). None of the measures of this lesson observes that edge. Administrative status, population density and light at night each draw it somewhere else, and in Vietnam the choice of measure changes the answer more than three decades of growth have changed any one of them. Lesson 2 compared several measures for Ho Chi Minh City; this lesson shows why the same care is needed for the country as a whole.

Exercise 5.1Where does Hanoi end?

Open the second view of Map 5.1. Find the urban centre of Hanoi and follow the land between it and the neighbouring provincial cities. Would you call that land urban or rural? Which of the three definitions matches your answer, and what would you need to know to decide?

Exercise 5.2Two instruments

On the left of Figure 5.2, read the lit area at the threshold of the World Bank in 1996, 2013, 2014 and 2024, and compute the yearly growth rate from 1996 to 2013 and from 2014 to 2024. Then compute it from 2012 to 2017 alone. Explain why the choice of years changes the conclusion.
